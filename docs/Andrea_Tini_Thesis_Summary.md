## 📌 Executive Summary
This thesis explores the conceptualization, development, and comprehensive evaluation of an **antidrone system utilizing high-performance, armed quadcopters**. Drawing inspiration from established missile interception algorithms, the study adapts these theories to the unique dynamics of multi-rotor drones. By developing a precise dynamic model, a fine-tuned flight controller, and several optimized guidance algorithms, the author proves that the **Optimal Guidance Law (OGL)** offers the highest efficiency for intercepting targets in both static and dynamic scenarios.

---

## 📖 Chapter 1: Introduction
The proliferation of commercially available drones poses significant security threats. Existing countermeasures, such as Israel's *Iron Dome*, are highly effective against missiles but are excessively costly (~$50M per battery) and oversized for drone interception. 
* **Proposed Solution:** Employing explosive-equipped quadcopters for rapid, cost-effective target obliteration.
* **Goal:** Intercept and neutralize hostile drones in the shortest possible time.

---

## ⚙️ Chapter 2: Dynamic Model
To simulate realistic physical behavior, a **non-linear mathematical model** of the drone was constructed in Simulink.
* **Translational Dynamics:** Incorporates propulsive thrust, gravitational pull, and aerodynamic drag forces. Uses Coriolis equations to account for the non-inertial nature of the body-fixed reference system.
* **Rotational Dynamics:** Models the roll ($\phi$), pitch ($	heta$), and yaw ($\psi$) torques generated by the asymmetric distribution of thrust and motor rotation across the quadcopter's rotors.

---

## 🎛️ Chapter 3: Flight Controller
The flight control software utilizes a **Cascade PID (Proportional-Integral-Derivative) Control System** to manage complex rotary-wing dynamics.
* **Architecture:** * *Outer Loop:* Controls vertical speed and velocity (slower dynamics).
  * *Inner Loop:* Controls attitude (pitch/roll) and heading (faster dynamics).
* **Motor Mixing:** Translates the desired rotational torques and total thrust into individual PWM (Pulse Width Modulation) signals for the four motors.
* **Testing:** Controllers were rigorously tested using step-change inputs, demonstrating stable, highly responsive performance without excessive oscillation.

---

## 🎯 Chapter 4: Guidance Controllers
The core of the thesis focuses on adapting traditional ballistic missile guidance algorithms for quadcopters. The algorithms were implemented and iteratively refined in 3D:

1. **Proportional Navigation (PN):** Commands acceleration proportional to the line-of-sight rate. Standard missile assumptions (e.g., non-zero constant velocity, acceleration strictly perpendicular to line-of-sight) caused singularities in drones. The algorithm was heavily modified to alter both velocity magnitude and direction using a "Time-to-Go" ($t_{go}$) metric.
2. **Augmented Proportional Navigation (APN):** Enhances PN by factoring in the target's acceleration, computing a "Zero Effort Miss" (ZEM) to proactively adjust the interceptor's trajectory.
3. **Optimal Guidance Law (OGL):** Acknowledges the drone's dynamic response delay (lag) using a first-order transfer function. It optimizes the trajectory using a Linear Quadratic (LQ) optimization problem, minimizing both the miss distance and the required control effort.
4. **PID Guidance:** A custom-built, purely PID-based guidance system that utilizes the relative position error between the target and interceptor to command velocity.

---

## 📊 Chapter 5: Results & Comparative Analysis
The guidance subsystems were pitted against static and moving targets in Simulink:

* **Static Targets:** **OGL drastically outperformed** the others, reaching the target in 1/12th the time without the severe altitude overshooting observed in PN, APN, and PID controllers. PN and APN performed identically since target acceleration was zero.
* **Moving Targets:** In randomized 3D scenarios (e.g., intercepting a cruising target drone), OGL again showed superior performance with the shortest interception times and no overshooting. 
* **Limitations:** PN and APN suffer from efficiency drops at extreme target velocities (above ~60 km/h) due to a lack of specific optimization. OGL remains robust even at high speeds (~90 km/h) with minor tuning adjustments to its penalty parameters.

---

## 🎓 Conclusions
The thesis successfully validates that **missile guidance algorithms can be translated into the drone domain**, provided that traditional missile-centric assumptions are discarded. 
* Removing the constraint that commanded acceleration must be perpendicular to the relative distance was a crucial breakthrough.
* Ultimately, the **Optimal Guidance Law (OGL)** proved to be the most robust, efficient, and precise method, making it a highly viable software foundation for autonomous drone-based interception systems.
